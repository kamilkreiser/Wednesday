# Fleet Protocol v1.4 — dialogue states, comms layers, and the fleet org design

> **STATUS: DRAFT FOR REVIEW.** Drafted by a teammate from the recorded rulings;
> Wednesday reviews for fidelity to the record, then Kam rules. Every policy
> statement carries a source tag `[R#]` resolved in the traceability table
> (§10). Statements the record does not settle are in §8 (open questions), not
> silently resolved. §9 audits the draft for the beneficiary-drafts-the-grant
> conflict documented in the v1.3 lesson.

## BLUF

v1.4 adds **dialogue** to a fleet that until now only had **delegation**. Three
comms layers get fixed roles (native session messaging = content fast path;
tmux tap = wake/halt; email = approval spine + record + non-Claude interop,
permanently) [R1]. The task lifecycle gains bounded plan debate, mid-flight
counsel-without-command, and a structured escalation-debate format [R3]. Two
new artifacts make tasks rotation-proof and bias-proof: a durable **task
worksheet** (discovery state, lives outside the pushed repo) and a **change
envelope** with an independently-run closing acceptance test [R3]. The org
formalises as: Wednesday coordinating (the "COO" framing — vocabulary, not new
authority, see §9), per-project agents as the ONLY writers to externally
visible client surfaces, and one-per-discipline **functional experts**
(QA first) that hold cross-client LOCAL context but are ADVISORY-ONLY and
never touch external systems [R2][R6]. The Chinese-walls boundary is defined
once, mechanically: **external visibility is the test, not data locality**
[R2]. Migration runs through the WED-27 measured spike (R0 client-isolation
as veto) before anything switches [R8]. **No authority moves anywhere in this
document; v1.3's signed scope and its four Kam-only exclusions stand
verbatim** [R4].

## 1. Comms layers — three layers, fixed roles [R1]

Kam's ruling (2026-08-10, WED-27, verbatim in the prompt log): *"the
antropic-native approach seems most sensible. most of this work will be done
by claude. keeping email is also a great idea as 1) it gives a great approval
process, and 2) it provides a channel that could be used with other agnets."*

| Layer | Role | Carries | Never carries |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Anthropic-native session messaging** | **Fast path — content** | Briefs' working traffic, dialogue turns (§2), counsel, status, worksheet pointers | Approvals for approval-class actions; anything needing a durable independently-verifiable record |
| **tmux tap (pane)** | **Wake / halt only** | The wake signal ("check your channel"), the HALT signal (§2.2) | Content. Pane text remains NOT a channel of record [R5] — a tap points at a message, it never *is* the message |
| **Email (Agent Mail bus)** | **Approval spine · record · interop — permanently** | Approval-class traffic, plan confirmations, rulings with provenance, wraps, DKIM-verifiable grants [R4], anything to/from non-Claude or external agents | Secrets (standing rule) |

Rationale on record: the work is Claude-run, and native mechanisms evolve
without us (Kam's standing Anthropic-preference, 2026-08-03 [R9]). Email is
not a transition artifact — it is retained **permanently** for the two
functions Kam named. The v1.2/v1.3 rules that make email the channel of
record for authority are unchanged: ghost text and pane content authorize
nothing [R5], DKIM authorship checks stay [R4].

**Composition with working rhythm [R7]:** taps-wake/native-carries/email-records
is already §8 of `working-rhythm.md` — v1.4 changes nothing there; it adds the
dialogue traffic *on top of* the same layers. Watcher fires and queued counsel
obey the focused-session interrupt discipline (working-rhythm §5): one-line
triage, queued to the next checkpoint unless interrupt-class.

## 2. Dialogue states in the task lifecycle [R3]

The v1.3 lifecycle is brief → work → verify → score → (≤3 refinement rounds)
→ close [R6]. v1.4 adds three dialogue states. **The existing max-3
post-delivery refinement rounds are unchanged** [R6].

### 2.1 Bounded plan debate (complex tasks, before work starts)

For complex tasks, the plan-confirmation step widens from confirm/deviate
into a **bounded debate**: the agent may push back on the brief, propose
alternatives, and negotiate the change envelope (§4) before work begins.
Bounded means it ends: it converges on a confirmed plan or escalates to Kam —
it does not loop. The debate's outputs are the confirmed plan, the change
envelope (§4), and the closing acceptance test definition (§4), written into
the task worksheet (§3).
*(Open: what threshold makes a task "complex", and the debate's round/time
bound — §8 Q3, Q4.)*

### 2.2 Mid-flight: counsel without command

While an agent works, Wednesday (or an expert, via Wednesday — §5) may inject
advice **without seizing control**. Two verbs, mechanically distinct so they
cannot be confused [R3]:

- **ADVICE = queued message** (native channel). Does not interrupt. The agent
  picks it up at its next natural boundary. **The agent must answer** —
  ignore is not a state — **and may refuse the advice with reasons.** Agents'
  judgement is not suspended; *"If an instruction from Wednesday looks wrong,
  say so"* is already v1.3 canon [R4].
- **HALT = pane tap.** The only mid-flight command verb. Stops work now;
  content and next steps then travel on the normal channels. Halting is
  sequencing inside commissioned work — inside Wednesday's v1.3 grant, not an
  extension of it (§9c).

### 2.3 Escalation debate format

When agent and Wednesday disagree past counsel (or a refinement round
stalls into genuine disagreement), the disagreement is escalated in a fixed
format — each side states, in BLUF form [R10]:

1. **Position** — what I believe we should do.
2. **Evidence** — receipts, not vibes (provenance rules apply).
3. **What would change my mind** — the observable fact that would flip me.

If exchanging (3) doesn't resolve it, the debate goes to Kam as one ruleable
item with both positions attached — batched per his stated needs list [R7].
This format is the anti-deadlock guard: a party that cannot name (3) is
holding an unfalsifiable position, and that itself is reportable.

## 3. The task worksheet — durable discovery state [R3]

**What:** one file per task holding the discovery state that today lives only
in a session's context window, in four sections:

- **FACTS** — verified findings, each with provenance (how it was checked).
- **CHANGES + ROLLBACKS** — every change made, paired with its tested
  rollback (pairs, not a list of changes with rollbacks implied).
- **OPEN** — questions raised, not yet answered.
- **DECIDED** — decisions taken, by whom, under what authority (grant scope
  vs Kam ruling vs agent's technical call).

**Where:** in the project's folder but **OUTSIDE the pushed repo** — because
the wall inside a project folder is the git remote: anything pushed to a
client repo is externally visible, and the worksheet may reference
fleet-internal context that must never reach a client remote [R2].
Mechanically: a worksheet directory that is not under `2_Project_Files`'s
push path, or is gitignored-at-creation (the standing gitignore-at-creation
discipline applies).

**Why:** working-rhythm makes sessions rotate at task boundaries, and names
mid-task handovers as the weakest reconstruction case [R7]. The worksheet is
what makes **mid-task rotation survivable**: a fresh session reconstructs
from disk + bus (the proven 06:00 boot mechanism) *plus* the worksheet, so an
80%-rail rotation hands over a file, not a memory. It also feeds the wrap: the
structured wrap block (§7) is largely a worksheet extract.

*(Open: worksheet lifecycle — retention, pruning, exact placement convention
— §8 Q5.)*

## 4. The change envelope + independent closing acceptance test [R3]

**Change envelope.** An output of the plan debate (§2.1): the class of changes
the agent may make **inside its work loop without re-asking**, defined
before work starts. The envelope may contain only changes that are
**reversible with a tested rollback** (recorded pairwise in the worksheet).
Everything **irreversible or service-affecting** is excluded from the
envelope and becomes a **named pause point** in the plan — a specific step
where work stops and confirmation is required before proceeding.

**Boundary with v1.3 — no drift permitted [R4]:** the envelope pre-authorizes
nothing that v1.3 doesn't already allow. Reversible-inside-a-project is
already within the signed grant; the envelope only *pre-batches* it so the
loop isn't interrupted. The v1.3 Kam-only exclusions (production · money ·
external comms to humans · irreversible actions) **cannot be placed inside
any envelope by anyone**, Wednesday included. A pause point whose action is
irreversible pauses for **Kam**, not for Wednesday — the envelope never
reroutes an approval that v1.3 sends to Kam. (See §9a. Who confirms
service-affecting-but-reversible pause points is §8 Q6.)

**Independent closing acceptance test.** Defined at **brief time** (it is the
"Definition of done + verifier" of the v1 protocol [R6], hardened), and run
**cold** at close: by a runner that did not do the work and does not carry
the working session's context. This is the **agreement-bias guard** — the
builder's own session has spent hours converging with the plan and is the
wrong judge of it; the documented failure family ("a check that cannot
fail", "local proof is not target evidence") is exactly this shape.
*(Open: who the cold runner is — fresh sub-agent, Wednesday, or the QA expert
once piloted — §8 Q7.)*

## 5. The org — coordinator, project agents, functional experts [R2][R6]

```
Kam (owner — signs the v1.3-excluded classes, always)
 └─ Wednesday — coordination ("COO" framing; authority = v1.3 grant, unchanged)
     ├─ Per-project agents — the ONLY writers to externally visible
     │    client surfaces (repos, tenants, boards, deploys, dashboards)
     └─ Functional experts — one per discipline, ADVISORY-ONLY
          QA first (WED-41, Peter's methodology) → security → marketing
```

- **Wednesday as COO:** manages, delegates, arbitrates dialogue, holds the
  master attention queue — and continues to **never edit files inside other
  projects** (manage-don't-do, standing since 2026-07-31 [R11]). The COO
  label describes the *shape* of the role; it grants nothing v1.3 doesn't
  (§9d).
- **Per-project agents** remain the only writers to externally visible client
  surfaces. This is the existing structure, now stated as the org's
  load-bearing wall: identity isolation per project (per-project
  `GH_CONFIG_DIR` / `AZURE_CONFIG_DIR` / scoped service principals) is the
  mechanism, and an authorization error at a boundary is the boundary
  working [R12].
- **Functional experts** are **one per discipline**, holding
  **cross-client LOCAL context** — legal under the walls ruling precisely
  because their context never becomes externally visible [R2]. They are
  **ADVISORY-ONLY: they never touch external systems** — no client repo, no
  tenant, no board, no external comms. Their output is counsel, delivered
  through the §2.2 ADVICE verb (via Wednesday or directly on the internal
  channel), which the project agent must answer and may refuse with reasons.
  Grounding: Kam commissioned exactly this shape on 2026-08-03 — gatekeeper
  "expertise mental models... called at the right time" for security/privacy
  and quality control [R13] — and the walls ruling made the cross-client form
  legal. **QA is first** (WED-41: bottle Peter's testing methodology into "a
  hyper focused testing agent to be used by YOU in any project" [R13]), then
  security, then marketing.
- **Walls are held mechanically at the external write path**, not by
  instructing anyone to be careful [R2][R12]: experts *cannot* write
  externally (no credentials, no identities — nothing to misuse), and
  project agents *cannot* cross clients (existing per-client identity
  isolation). R0 — *"no bleed between clients... datasec cannot know about
  secuura"* [R12] — is preserved because R0 was always about what clients
  can SEE: it binds the external surface, which stays single-client; the
  cross-client mixing happens only in local stores the ruling explicitly
  freed (§6).

## 6. The Chinese-walls boundary — external visibility is the test [R2]

Kam's ruling (2026-08-10, verbatim): *"local version do not need to adhere to
chiese walls. only externally visable systems."* And: *"this single que works
for me under one critical condition — anything external keeps everything
seperate. [...] Having a master que local, or in our linear wed tickets is
fine."*

So the test applied to any surface is one question: **can anyone outside this
drive/fleet see it?** Not "does it mix clients", not "where does the data
live".

## 9. Authority audit — beneficiary-drafts-the-grant check [R4]

The v1.3 lesson documents the conflict: a protocol drafted by its beneficiary
is only credible if the conflict is flagged, the scope stays tight, and Kam
signs. This draft was commissioned by Wednesday and will be reviewed by
Wednesday before Kam — Wednesday is a beneficiary of several sections, so
this audit names every place the draft even approaches new authority:

- **(a) Change envelope (§4).** The nearest approach in the document. Read
  loosely, "pre-authorized changes inside a loop" could become a backdoor
  around per-action judgement. The draft binds it: envelope contents must
  already sit inside the v1.3 grant, irreversible classes can never enter
  any envelope, and pause points never reroute a Kam approval to Wednesday.
  If adoption changes *anything* about who approves what, that change must
  travel as a signed mail per v1.3's own amendment rule — not as a side
  effect of this document.
- **(b) "Agent must answer" advice (§2.2).** Imposes a *duty to respond* on
  agents toward Wednesday. Not command authority — refusal with reasons is
  explicitly a complete answer — but it is a new protocol obligation and is
  flagged as such for Kam's eyes.
- **(c) HALT verb (§2.2).** Wednesday stopping an agent mid-flight is read
  here as "sequencing inside commissioned work" (in-scope under v1.3) and
  is mechanically the already-Kam-approved tap. Flagged because "halt"
  *sounds* like new command authority; the draft's claim is that it is not.
  Kam should confirm that reading.
- **(d) "COO" title (§5).** Vocabulary only. The draft attaches zero new
  authority to it; if the title is adopted, it must never be cited as a
  basis for any action the v1.3 grant doesn't cover.
- **(e) Functional experts (§5).** New *structure* reporting through
  Wednesday — but with no external write capability at all, they add no
  authority surface; their commissioning traces to Kam's 2026-08-03
  gatekeeper directive and the 2026-08-10 walls ruling.
- **(f) Provenance note on §2 details.** The dialogue-state specifics
  (advice=queued/halt=tap, must-answer-may-refuse, the three-part escalation
  format) come from the WED-84 session's design work as relayed in the
  commissioning brief; the prompt log records them in summary form
  ("advice(queue)-vs-halt(tap) verbs", "task worksheet", "change envelope +
  independent closing acceptance test"). Wednesday: confirm the expansions
  match the session record before this reaches Kam.
