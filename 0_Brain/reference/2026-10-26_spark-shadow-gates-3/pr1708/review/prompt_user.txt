===== TICKET =====
KS-1728 — Shell suites grade with `printf ... | grep -q` under pipefail: two unrelated suites failed a loaded preflight with "printf: write error: Broken pipe" (150 such lines in 29 files) [In Progress]

## BLUF

Two unrelated shell suites each failed once inside preflight leg 14 of a pre-push, on the same tell, `printf: write error: Broken pipe`, while the machine was heavily loaded. Both grade a result with `printf '%s' "$out" | grep -qF ...` under `set -uo pipefail`. Both passed every time on a quiet machine (15 of 15 runs). **The failure was NOT reproduced under load, so the mechanism below is the explanation that fits, not a proven one.** The same line shape appears at 150 places in 29 files.

## What was seen

Two pushes ran their pre-push hook at the same time on one macOS laptop (12 CPUs). A Docker Desktop VM was at about 920% CPU and the load average read 98 / 100 / 98 at 00:45Z and my later readings of the 1-minute figure were 74 (00:52Z), 58 (01:12Z), 39 (01:13Z) and 6.65 (01:18Z), after Docker Desktop was restarted. Leg 14 took 947 s and 1007 s; the same leg took 553 s on an unloaded run earlier. Each push was a branch off develop `28dac5c12ba5` carrying a two-file change that touches neither suite. Both pushes were refused, `PREFLIGHT FAILED on leg(s) 14 - (13/16 legs ran)`, every other leg passing.

`check_shared_relink.test.sh` (push started 00:47:52Z, `shell suites: 90 passed, 1 failed, 1 skipped (of 92)`):

The captured output printed under the FAIL line contains the wanted text, so the rule fired and the grade was wrong. Line 49 is `if [ -n "$want_out" ] && ! printf '%s' "$out" | grep -qF -- "$want_out"; then`.

* `ks1355_slot_blind_launchers.test.sh`, 5 consecutive runs, same env, load 3.5 to 4.9, tree `b5767aa4c527` (tree identical to develop `28dac5c12ba5`): **5 of 5 passed, 23 passed / 0 failed, 0 "Broken pipe".**

## Not measured

The failure was not reproduced under load: no deliberate stress was run (it would have disturbed live pushes), so there is no measured failure rate under load. Only two failures were seen, one per suite, in one window. The mechanism is inferred from the shape: `grep -q` exits at its first match, a `printf` still writing to that pipe gets EPIPE, and `pipefail` turns the pipeline's status into failure, so a MATCH is graded as no match. It is a race between two processes, which fits "only when the machine is slow".

## Update 2026-10-26 02:20Z: a third suite, at moderate load, and a measurement that weakens the first explanation

**Third occurrence.** A third push was refused at leg 14 with the same tell. Push of the KS-1513 branch, started 01:53:06Z, ended `rc 1` after 1081 s: `shell suites: 90 passed, 1 failed, 1 skipped (of 92)`, FAILED `preflight_deps.test.sh`, which printed

```
.../scripts/__tests__/preflight_deps.test.sh: line 443: printf: write error: Broken pipe
.../scripts/__tests__/preflight_deps.test.sh: line 444: printf: write error: Broken pipe
FAIL KS-1638: that refusal SHORT-CIRCUITS — a red suite present, still not run
       want "HOOKCTL-REFUSED/1", got "OTHER/1"
preflight_deps: 60 passed, 1 failed
```

Lines 443 and 444 are consecutive `elif printf '%s' "$out" | grep -q '...'` verdict tests; in this cell the text for 443 (`could not derive the pre-push hook's own control`) sits at byte 1871 and the text for 444 (`de-slotted`) at byte 1821 of `$out`, so both match early and both pipelines failed, which sent the cell to the `OTHER` verdict. **This one was NOT in the 60 to 98 load window:** leg 14 took 662 s (against 553 s unloaded), and my readings of the 1-minute load average over the push were between 4.0 and 13.

**Quiet-box runs of this suite:** 6 of 6 passed on that exact tree (`716e26290155`, 61 passed / 0 failed, 0 "Broken pipe"), load about 6.

**What the measurement does to the explanation.** Using the suite's own `PREFLIGHT_FIXTURE_OUT` capture on the failing cell, `$out` is **2848 bytes**, 62 lines. That is far below the macOS pipe buffer (`st_blksize` 16384), so a single `write()` of it cannot meet a closed reader. The "large output, early match" form of the race is therefore NOT what happened here; for it to occur, bash 3.2's `printf` would have to deliver the string to `grep` in several small writes. I tried to reproduce that on the real captured output: 3 runs of 3000 iterations of `set -uo pipefail; printf '%s' "$out" | grep -q "could not derive the pre-push hook's own control"`, load about 8 to 10, **0 of 9000 reported no-match.** Not reproduced.

**Tally of pushes of mine that reached leg 14 since 00:43Z:** 6; refused by this tell: 3 (the KS-1391 and KS-1560 first attempts, and the KS-1513 push above); passed: 3 (the KS-1560 and KS-1391 second attempts, and KS-1289). A seventh push, KS-1716, ran no preflight leg. The refused ones were three different suites. So the suites are sometimes fragile on this machine under contention from several simultaneous preflights; the mechanism is unproven.
