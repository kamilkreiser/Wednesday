## BLUF for the owner (plain words)

1. **The rebuild is real, and most of it holds.** Everything the three earlier checks broke holds when I attack it again: one fake relay number no longer stalls anything; one fake packet no longer makes your computer resend everything; a lost "I have room again" message no longer holds messages for ever; a damaged file is reported to the assistant; nothing reached the assistant twice without a label; the safety-words "no" still holds across crashes; a peer that claims absurd numbers is clamped and the session carries on. When the network or the relay misbehaves in a session that is already running, the two computers were talking again within 16 to 52 seconds in every case I measured (TESTED), inside the two-minute promise.
2. **One hole stops it, and it is in the first minute of every session.** Right after two people pair, the relay has to say "the other person is here" once. If the relay withholds that ONE line, and from then on behaves perfectly, the two computers sit side by side, both connected to the relay, and never start talking: both people's messages stay queued, both screens say "not connected", and nothing changes until one program is restarted or the network drops. I watched this for 150 seconds of perfect relay behaviour, 6 times in 8 tries, with nothing delivered (the other two were rescued by a timing accident at the start, and when I removed that accident it stalled 4 times in 4; TESTED); the pure logic shows no clock at all after 10 simulated minutes (TESTED). That is exactly the kind of fault the rebuild exists to remove ("nothing a relay does may stall a session for good"), in a starting state the builder's tests never produce.
3. **The fix looks small** (a just-paired session must count as "no channel yet", so the existing 15-second clocks run), but it is a change to the recovery code, so by the rules it needs a fix and one more check.
4. Smaller things: two source comments carry a person's name; one old red-control row goes red for a side reason; the docs promise a 15-second clock covers "no announcement" without saying it does not cover a brand-new session; several clocks' exact values are not pinned by any test; the clamp that stops a hostile peer crashing your daemon has no test of its own (it works: I tested it). The "64 waiting calls" weakness is exactly as described: only a program on your own computer can do it.
