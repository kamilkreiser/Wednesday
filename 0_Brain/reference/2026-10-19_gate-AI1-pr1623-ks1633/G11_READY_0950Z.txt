## Cause

The route anchored before it wrote its `documents` row, and the anchoring service persists its `anchor_store` row **before** it answers 202 (`anchoring/src/index.ts:507` precedes `:543`). So any failure of the certification write left an anchor naming no document — an orphan anchor, permanently, from a legitimate authenticated flow, and orphan anchors are the rows whose anchored identity another registration can claim (the KS-1612 round-2 Major). The row is now written first, and the one refusal between the two steps compensates it away before the 503, so **every response stays byte-identical**.

## The two things you asked the gate to test

**1. The `withTenant` delete.** `documents` is FORCE RLS under `tenant_isolation`, whose USING clause reads the **transaction-local** GUC `app.current_tenant_id` (`set_config(…, true)`). Measured as `secuura_app` on a migrated scratch database: **outside** that transaction the DELETE answers **`DELETE 0` and raises nothing, with the row still present**; **inside** it, **`DELETE 1`**. So a compensating delete written the obvious way removes nothing and still looks like success — **my first version did exactly that, and the cell caught it, not my reading.** `deleteCertification` therefore runs through `withTenant` (which sets both GUCs, KS-458) and **returns the row count**, and the route treats **anything but 1 as a failed compensation**: a discarded count would hide the very residue the log line exists to report. En route I also mis-keyed my own control to `app.tenant_id`; it "passed" by deleting nothing, and the row count exposed it.

Worth the gate's attention specifically: `verifiable_credentials.document_id` is ON DELETE **NO ACTION** while every other child of a fresh certification cascades, so a certification that has grown a credential **throws** here rather than cascading. That is why the caller handles a throw instead of swallowing it, and why the DELETE-FAILS arm is not hypothetical.

**2. The crash window.** The accepted path writes the row twice, and the provisional row carries the state a FAILED anchor carries. If the process dies between the two writes the row is left saying `failed` (off-chain-only) — conservative and honest. Your reason for preferring this over a statusless `pending-onchain` provisional is in the PR body as a **known, accepted residual**: a crashed request under shape 2 would show *pending* with nothing ever updating it, which is the defect Kam's (b) closed. The cost accepted is a sub-second window in which a request whose anchor is about to succeed reads as off-chain-only. **It is reasoned from the code, not reproduced** — I have not killed a process mid-request, and I say so in the PR.
