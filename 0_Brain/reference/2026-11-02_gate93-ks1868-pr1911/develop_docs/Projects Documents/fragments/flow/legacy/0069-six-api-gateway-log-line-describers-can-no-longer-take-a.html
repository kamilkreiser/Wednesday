80. Six api-gateway log-line describers can no longer take a request down (KS-1771)

What this changes. KS-1771 gives six sibling log lines in services/api-gateway the guard PR 1754 gave the document-lookup catch (KS 1524 a): audit-export.ts fail500 and its 502 catch, batch.ts fail500, notifications.ts fail500, the audit-log catch in platform.ts, and the document-types catch in verification.ts. Each described the caught value, and wrote the log line, inside the catch with no try of its own.
A behaviour change on a request path. A thrown value that cannot be described (a message getter that throws, a Proxy whose getPrototypeOf or ownKeys trap throws, a revoked Proxy) or a logger that throws escaped the catch. Each site now describes the value in its own try (fallback thrown value that could not be described) and logs in another, so its constant answer is always sent. The line is unchanged for every value that could be described before.
Held, not touched. The five matching lines in originate (KS 1772), platform.ts everywhere except the audit-log catch, and the authentication posture of the document-lookup route (KS 1773).

Measured at base ba9e8fad7f9d, 2026-10-27, Kam’s Mac. Every cell reports what the request received, so the sites separate.
No answer at all, 5 of 5 inputs each: audit-export.ts fail500, batch.ts fail500, the audit-log catch in platform.ts and the document-types catch in verification.ts. Their handlers are async and Express 4 does not catch the rejection. The gateway survives unhandled rejections on purpose (KS 546), so the request is simply never answered. The 20 unhandled rejections are these four sites times five inputs.
A different answer, 5 of 5 each: the 502 catch in audit-export.ts falls to the handler’s outer catch, so fail500 answers 500 under another log context instead of the 502. In notifications.ts the six handlers are synchronous, so the throw reaches Express and the gateway’s errorHandler answers in place of the route’s constant 500.

80.3 What the cells prove, and what they do not

The route’s answer, not the process. The cells prove each route sends its constant answer for those five inputs and logs the fallback line; at the audit-log catch the answer is the degraded 200 (the audit_logs row, the platform half dropped). They do not say what an unhandled rejection does to a running gateway.
The empty catch is deliberate. The catch around each log call is empty, with its reason written beside it: if the logger itself fails there is no other channel to report it on, and the caller must still get its answer. It is a named exception, not a silenced error.
One injection caveat. The audit-log catch is driven through the response-body read (await r.json()), because fetchWithTimeout evaluates err instanceof Error in its own catch and would replace two of the four hostile shapes with a plain TypeError before they reached the catch under test.
