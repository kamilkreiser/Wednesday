. Thread-mint waits at most 30 s for the wallet and answers 503 before signing, takes its coins from the wallet view, and is recorded in the view from signing (KS-1384 F0c)

What this changes.The blocker it removes (the fourth review’s B1). Before this, putting POST /api/anchors/thread-mint behind the wallet lock would have made it worse. The single-anchor path holds the lock through its ~27-minute confirmation poll; originate calls thread-mint with a bare fetch and gives up long before that; so a queued mint would sign and pay after its caller had gone, and no registry row would name the paid NFT.
A bounded wait and three refusals before signing (services/anchoring/src/threadMintSpend.ts, new; the handler in index.ts). The lock is taken with withWalletLockWithin and 30 s, well under the caller’s timeout. When the wait expires nothing is signed and the answer is 503 (B1a). With no spendable coin the answer is 503 at once; thread-mint never enters the anchors’ 2 h wait (B1b). A caller whose socket has closed stops the mint immediately before signing (B1c). These three, and a wallet that is not initialised, are the only answers mapped to 503.
Coins from the view (cardano/threadTokenMint.ts). The seed is the first pass-1 candidate of at least 5 ADA, and the mint’s inputs are preset to the view’s candidates (never empty), so a coin a live anchor is spending is never the seed or an input (M-2). Before this, the seed came from a fresh provider read.
Recorded at signing, confirmed outside the lock. The mint has no database row and no write-ahead, so it enters the view at signing from its signed body: inputs, collateral, collateral return (M-3). The next anchor build avoids those coins while the mint is live. Its confirmation runs outside the lock and tells the view valid_contract: a valid mint gets its collateral back and its input stays claimed (M-1). A refused submit frees the coins at once; a lost reply keeps them claimed. A signed body with no TTL is treated as valid until its inputs are spent (cardano/transaction.ts).
Not in this change: the OFF-by-default switch and the owner rule on the wallet-spending routes (KS-1819 and KS-1820’s switch halves). The thread-mint hunk is kept small so that work can merge over it.

.1 What proves it

Cells, each with its mutant.New cells, 10 (ks1384-f0c-thread-mint.test.ts). (B1a) an anchor holds the lock for 6 minutes: thread-mint gives up at 30 s and nothing is signed, then or later. (B1b) every coin claimed: no coin after one fetch, and the mint is never called. (B1c) the caller went away: nothing signed, submitted or admitted. Then (M-2), (seed), (M3t, p5), (p8), (out) twice, and (503). The module is new, so each cell is proven by its mutant.
Mutants: 9 written, each parsed before it ran; 9 killed. (B1a) a 1000-times-longer lock wait; (B1b) mint anyway with no candidate; (B1c) no caller check before signing; (503) the caller-gone refusal not mapped to 503, 2 red; (M-2) offer every fetched coin, not pass 1; (M3t) never admit the mint to the view, 4 red; (out) every failed submit read as refused; (p8) no confirmation outside the lock; (seed) a 1 ADA seed threshold; every other mutant 1 red. B1’s cell is red against its mutant (the lock wait made 1000 times longer: 1 of 10, the (B1a) cell only) and green with the change.
