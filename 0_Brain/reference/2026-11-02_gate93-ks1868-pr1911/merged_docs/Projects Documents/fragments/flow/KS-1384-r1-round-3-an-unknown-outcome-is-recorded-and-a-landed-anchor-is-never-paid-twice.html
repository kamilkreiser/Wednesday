. The document anchor retry route: a re-anchor whose outcome is unknown is recorded, so a landed anchor is never paid for twice (KS-1384 R1 round 3)

What this changes.The fee it closes (QA gate 61, G61-F1). Round 2 bounded the post at 10 s and treated a timeout as an unknown outcome: it kept the claim and answered 503 with Retry-After 600, but recorded nothing. Anchoring writes its row BEFORE it spends the fee (anchoring index.ts: await dbPersistAnchor at line 529, processAnchor at 577, the 202 at 581), so a slow post often lands. By the time the retry came, the row had confirmed, and a confirmed row read as “nothing in flight”, so the retry paid a second fee. Gate 61 measured it: 1 post at round 1, 2 at round 2 (its P3b and P3c).
The marker (documentRepo.ts, new markReanchorPostUnknown). On the unknown-outcome path the blob gains retryPostUnknownAt and retryPostUnknownToken (the claim token); the claim is still kept. The same write drops retryClaimMadeBlob, so no later release can delete a blob that records a post which may have landed. It is guarded on a blob that is not confirmed, not on the token, so a holder that stalled past its lease still records the fact.
A marked document never posts on a row (reanchorInFlight.ts, decision maybe-landed). A claimant that reads the marker treats ANY anchoring row for the document, confirmed and failed included, as that post’s: 409 naming the row, the claim released, the marker kept. Only “no row” lets it post, read under a claim that could only be taken after the marked post’s lease ended. That is safe because anchoring persists the row before the fee and before any 2xx. A pending blob with the marker and no claim (what a release leaves on a blob the claim made) stays claimable.
The re-check (confirmReanchorClaim) also requires that the marker is exactly the one the claimant read, and removes it as it renews the lease: a marker written after the read means a post may have landed that the decision never saw. It now also refuses a revoked or deleted row and a confirmed blob (G61-F3: before, a revoke that landed during the in-flight read was followed by a fee). A 2xx whose headers arrived is accepted (postReanchor): the post has 10 s to its headers, then 10 s more to its body. A body that arrives is the normal success; one that is lost, or is not a JSON object, is recorded as an unknown outcome (503, Retry-After 600, saying anchoring accepted it). A JSON body that names no anchor id is a success that also records the marker, so the blob, which learns no anchorId, stays covered by the marker rule (Wednesday’s ruling, option b; anchoring always answers the id it persisted, but this does not depend on it). Round 2’s single signal also cut off the body and threw such a 202 away. The whole budget is 25 s (read 5, headers 10, body 10), 1/24 of the lease.
Who sees a different answer: a retry of a document whose last post timed out and has since landed is refused (409) instead of paying again; a 202 with a slow body now succeeds; a revoked or resolver-confirmed document is no longer anchored by a holder already in its read. Nothing else on the route changes.

.3 What this does not prove

Stated, not claimed.A holder frozen for longer than the lease between its post and its marker write leaves no marker; a request that reaches anchoring and stalls there for longer than the lease before its persist commits could land after a later claimant read “no row”. Neither is measured (the cells force stalls by backdating the claim, as gates 58 and 61 did).
A post that fails in another way after anchoring has persisted its row (a connection reset mid-request) keeps R1’s failure handling, unchanged since round 1; R2a’s landed-anchor answer covers a confirmed row of that kind. A marked document stays refused on a failed row until R2b’s resolver decides it: money-safe, not yet live.
