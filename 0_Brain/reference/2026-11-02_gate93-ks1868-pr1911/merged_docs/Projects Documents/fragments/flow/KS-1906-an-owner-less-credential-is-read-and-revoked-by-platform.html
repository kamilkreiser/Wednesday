. An owner-less credential is read and revoked by platform administrators of its tenant, not by tenant administrators (KS-1906)

What this changes. Kam ruled on card secuura-ks1851-ownerless-credentials-who-1031, option (a): “a — Yes, extend your rule to credentials”. “Your rule” is his KS 1695 rule (b), whose card admits only the connector that created a record, plus platform administrators, and says “Records with no creation record … become unreadable to S too”. Before, a vc-issuer credential with no recorded owner followed the KS 1116 ruling: a tenant administrator (ORG_ADMIN or SYSTEM_ADMIN) of the row’s own tenant.
Credentials now decide with mayReadCredential (ownership.ts). It is mayRead for an owned row, and for a row with no recorded owner it applies ownerlessCredentialReadable:
  • a platform administrator: isPlatformRole from the shared package, SYSTEM_ADMIN or SUPER_ADMIN, ASCII case fold only, as originate’s certification read uses it;
  • only when the token’s tenant equals the row’s created_by_tenant_id. vc_credentials_store has no row-level security, so this comparison is the only cross-tenant wall. A row with no recorded tenant (every row from before migration 054) is readable by nobody, as before;
  • no connector. An owner-less credential has no creation record, and one a connector creates is stamped with that connector’s userId, so it is owned and its creator reads it through mayRead. The card’s option text also names “Platform S connectors”; that disagreement is a card for Kam (secuura-ks1906-ownerless-credentials-connectors-1101), and until he rules connectors are refused.
All the credential reads and the revoke use it: GET /api/credentials/:id, POST /api/credentials/:id/revoke, the by-hash read and the list (database and memory paths each), and the list’s SQL filter, whose text is unchanged; only its third parameter now comes from the new rule. Presentations keep mayRead and the KS 1116 ruling: Kam’s words name credentials only.
Behaviour notes. For an owner-less credential of the caller’s tenant: ORG_ADMIN 200 becomes 404; SUPER_ADMIN 404 becomes 200; SYSTEM_ADMIN stays 200, now as a platform administrator (the KS 1116 card called it a tenant administrator, the shared isPlatformRole classes it as platform); a connector stays 404. Owned rows are untouched: a platform administrator still cannot read or revoke a credential someone else created. Unmeasured: whether any platform administrator user carries a tenant; one without a tenant reads no owner-less credential.
