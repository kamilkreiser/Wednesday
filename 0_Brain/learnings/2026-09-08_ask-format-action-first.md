# When I need Kam to do something, the ASK goes first and it is spelled out as steps — "I need you to do X. The rationale is Y. My recommendation was… Other options include…"

**The operative case, so the headline matches it:** Wednesday is writing anything to Kam that
requires **his hands** — a command, a click, a ruling, a change to a file only he owns.
**The first thing on the page is the action, stated as an instruction, with the exact steps.**
Not the problem, not the context, not what was found. He can always read down for the why; he
cannot act on a why.

**His words, verbatim (2026-09-08 15:34):**
> *"The comments you provide are fantastic. However, can we move closer to the bluff principle?
> And it would work much better if you need me to do something for you to spell out exactly the
> steps or what you want me to do. So I need you to do X. The rationale is Y. My recommendation
> was the following. Other options include"*

## The shape, in his own order

1. **I NEED YOU TO DO X** — the action, imperative, with the **literal** command, URL, file path
   or card id. Multiple actions get numbered, one block each.
2. **RATIONALE** — why it needs him specifically, and what it costs to wait.
3. **MY RECOMMENDATION** — named as mine.
4. **OTHER OPTIONS** — stated fairly, including the do-nothing, with its real cost.

## Why this is a real correction and not a formatting nicety

The 2026-08-06 format (Client/Project · problem · options · recommendation) has the right FIELDS
and the wrong ORDER: it opens with the problem, so **the ask is the fourth thing he meets.** He is
often reading on a phone, between things, and — since 2026-09-08 15:23 — **listening rather than
reading**, because the panel now speaks the whole message. An ask buried three paragraphs down is
an ask he has to hold in his head while the rest plays.

**The timing is the uncomfortable part and it belongs here:** he asked for this eleven minutes
after telling me to make the voice read everything. Removing the truncation made length cost him
real seconds, and Wednesday's very next message was longer than any before it. **He felt the change
immediately and I did not.** [[2026-08-06_bluf-write-for-the-reader]] said his reading time is the
scarce resource; his LISTENING time is scarcer, and it is the one I just made expensive.

## How to apply

1. **Action first, always, when his hands are required.** If the message needs nothing from him,
   say *"no action needed"* in the first line instead — the same discipline pointed the other way.
2. **"Spell out exactly the steps" means literal and pasteable** — the whole command on its own
   line, the direct URL, the exact setting and its file
   ([[2026-09-07_hand-kam-the-link-not-the-instruction]] is this rule's sibling: never make him
   go and find the thing).
3. **Consolidate his open asks rather than dripping them.** Three numbered blocks in one message
   beat three messages, because each message is now read aloud in full.
4. **Keep the recommendation labelled as mine and the alternatives honest**, including the
   do-nothing and its real cost ([[2026-08-16_an-ask-without-a-default-is-an-indefinite-hold]]).
5. **Length is now a cost with a unit.** Before adding a paragraph, ask whether he would want it
   read to him. Detail he might want goes in a file with a pointer, not into the message.
