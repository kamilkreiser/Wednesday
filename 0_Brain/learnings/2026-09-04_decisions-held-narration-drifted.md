# When Kam says I have been sloppy, classify my errors before answering — mine were all NARRATION, and the rulings held

**The operative case:** Kam has noticed a drop in quality and is asking why. **Do not list the
mistakes and do not reach for the context window. Classify them.** The answer he needs is
*which kind* of thing degraded, because that tells him what he can still trust and what he
cannot — and it tells me where to apply care.

## The case

Late in a long session Kam asked, plainly: *"there's been a lack of consistency or thought.
Is this the size of the context window that's impacting you, or something else?"*

The tempting answers were both wrong. **"It's the context window"** is a mechanism claim I had
not measured, offered as an excuse. **A list of apologies** would have told him nothing he did
not already know.

**What the classification actually showed, and it surprised me:**

**Every single thing I had to walk back that night was a QUANTITY or a CHARACTERISATION.**
"Most of the space on your travel drive" (13 GB against 1.5 TiB free — under 1%). "Over an
hour of copying" (the hour was *scanning*; the copy was twenty minutes). "Nothing is broken
meanwhile" about a leaked token — **wrong because I read its power from its NAME**. Three
searches that matched the word rather than the thing. Twice, a zero from a search that had
been killed mid-run.

**Not one of the rulings drifted.** The QA gate's conditions, reopening RD-245 rather than
filing a successor, pinning the branch under review, refusing to edit Kam's sync profile,
refusing to touch another client's credentials until he said so, holding the merge back to
Peter — all consistent, all still correct on re-reading.

**So: the decisions held and the narration drifted. That is one fault, not six.**

## Why the distinction is worth more than the apology

**It is diagnostic rather than confessional.** "I made mistakes today" tells Kam to discount
everything. **"My descriptions were loose and my rulings were not"** tells him precisely what
survives, and it is checkable — he can go and read the rulings.

**It also locates the cause.** Memory degradation looks like forgetting decisions and
contradicting yourself; that did not happen. What happened is that I answered a fast
conversation at conversational speed and did the measurement *afterwards*. **That is a
speed/care tradeoff, not a capacity limit** — and the ledger already held three instances of
it from earlier the same day, which is the proof it is a habit and not that evening's context.

**And it protects the honest use of the real instrument.** There is a standing rule that a
session cannot judge its own degradation, which is exactly why the tripwire is a number and
not a feeling. **If I spend "it's my context" on an occasion where it is not true, the one
time it IS true nobody will believe it.**

## How to apply

1. **When Kam questions my quality, classify before answering.** Sort the errors into kinds —
   rulings, measurements, descriptions, omissions — and report which kind moved. **A list is
   not an answer; a pattern is.**
2. **Never offer the context window as a cause unless the statusline supports it AND the error
   shape fits.** Say the number, say what the shape does and does not look like, and say
   plainly when I do not know.
3. **THE RULE THIS PRODUCES, and it is the operative output: no quantity and no
   characterisation reaches Kam unless the measurement is in the same breath.** If I cannot
   measure it in that breath, the honest word is **"unmeasured"** — which is a real answer, and
   I have been treating it as a failure.
4. **Do not over-flagellate.** He asked for a diagnosis, not contrition. Naming what held is
   part of an honest answer, not self-defence — and a report that only lists faults is as
   miscalibrated as one that hides them.
5. **Prefer his framing back to him.** He said "consistency or thought". Consistency is about
   the rulings; thought is about the care in the sentence. **Answering in his own two
   categories is more useful than substituting mine.**

## The sibling finding: a reversal costs trust unless the FIRST call showed its reasoning

Part of what read as inconsistency was a genuine reversal — I assigned a task to a successor
seat, then took it back twenty minutes later. **The reversal was correct** (the facts changed,
and one leg of my original reasoning was wrong). **It still read badly, and that is on the
first call, not the second.**

**The fix: a decision that states what would change it converts a later reversal from
inconsistency into evidence the system is working.** *"I am assigning this away because it will
not fit; what would change my mind is if the branch pin turns out to block code work"* — said
first, the reversal is the mechanism operating. Said only afterwards, it is a wobble.
