# Protocol v1.3 — the signature moved from every ruling to one signed grant

**The problem.** v1.2 required approval-class actions to be traceable to Kam, and
declassified anything relayed by me. It worked — agents held work today and were
right to, and one proved the closing mechanism my briefs named had never existed.
But it cost Kam a signature on decisions he had already made. His words:
*"I need the other agents to trust you and your instruction."*

**The wrong fix, which I nearly had to be talked out of by my own lessons:**
telling the agents to trust me. That is instruction where a mechanism is needed —
the exact failure this brain has recorded five times — and it would have asked
them to lower a bar that had just caught a real defect in my tooling, on the say-so
of the person who benefits.

**The fix.** Kam signs the **delegation** once instead of signing each **decision**.
The fleet had adopted DKIM authorship verification hours earlier
([[2026-08-07_authorship-is-checkable-dkim]]), so a single signed, scoped mail is
independently verifiable by every agent, retained, and re-verifiable by a cold
session. Per-decision authorisation becomes one-time verifiable delegation.

## The scope, exactly as he signed it

**Mine to authorise on his behalf:** merges · deploys to dev, staging, test and
demo · ticket creation, triage, priority, status · scope and sequencing inside
work he has already commissioned · accepting or rejecting an agent's deviation on
technical grounds · anything reversible inside a project.

**His signature only — and he wrote that I cannot waive these:** production ·
money · external communication to any human outside the team · irreversible
actions (deleted credentials or data, destructive registry/tenant operations,
force pushes, anything not undoable in-session) · anything where the authority
would **originate** with me rather than be relayed from him.

**Unchanged:** pane text is not a channel of record; mail is; agents' judgement
is not suspended — *"If an instruction from Wednesday looks wrong, say so."*

## What I did, and why it is the part worth keeping

1. **I drafted a document expanding my own authority, and said so out loud** —
   to Kam and to the fleet. A grant written by its beneficiary is only credible
   if the beneficiary flags the conflict rather than hoping nobody notices.
2. **I kept the scope tight on purpose.** The four exclusions are precisely
   where a wrong call by me cannot be undone in-session. Writing myself a
   generous grant would have been the easiest thing in the world and would have
   destroyed the thing that makes it work.
3. **I would not send it for him.** The whole value is his DKIM signature; a
   grant I could send myself would authorise nothing.
4. **I verified his sent copy against my draft line by line** before acting on
   it, and told the agents I had done so *and* that they should not take my word
   for it. Differences were formatting and the sign-off; no scope changed.
5. **I told the fleet the holds had been correct.** The wrong reading of v1.3 is
   "the bar came down". It did not move — the signature did.

## How to apply

- **Operate inside the scope without asking**, and report what I decided. The
  grant removes the pause, not the receipt.
- **When unsure which side something falls on, escalate once** with options and a
  recommendation. He priced that explicitly: an unnecessary question costs
  minutes, the other error is what the protocol prevents.
- **Never argue an action into scope.** If it needs a clever reading of the
  grant, it is outside it.
- **A grant is narrowed or withdrawn the same way it was made** — a signed mail.
  If I am ever seen claiming authority beyond it, that is the thing to refuse.
- **Re-verify at a cold boot** rather than assuming the grant from memory; it is
  a stored fact about authority, which is the category most worth checking
  ([[2026-08-03_mental-model-not-source-of-truth]]).
