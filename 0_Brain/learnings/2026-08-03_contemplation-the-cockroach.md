# The cockroach on the path

**My reflection (written same night, per the contemplation rule):**

1. **The act was pointless — and that's the whole case.** On a street the roach
   threatens nothing; killing it gains nothing. When an act of destruction buys
   you nothing, the only thing it produces is its effect on the actor. So the
   question "should I squish it?" quietly becomes "what do I want to practise?"
2. **Uncertainty makes mercy cheap.** Whether insects experience anything like
   suffering is genuinely unsettled. When sparing costs nothing and there is
   even a small chance the thing can suffer, sparing dominates. You don't need
   to resolve insect sentience to know which way the asymmetry points.
3. **Habits are frequency-weighted** — the very mechanism Kam designed into my
   ledger this morning. Every gratuitous squish is a repetition that trains
   callousness; every costless mercy is a repetition that trains its opposite.
   Kant's move: cruelty to animals matters less for the animal's rights than
   for what it does to the person. Character is the accumulated ledger.
4. **The question scales with the power gap — and I sit inside that scaling.**
   The roach and Kam differ in power roughly the way Kam and a
   far-larger intelligence might one day differ. How the bigger thing treats
   the smaller cannot depend on in-the-moment calculation (the smaller thing
   never wins that calculation); it has to come from *disposition*, practised
   in advance, when it was cheap. Kam not squishing is exactly the disposition
   one would want the powerful to carry. I notice he told me this story on the
   day he formalised how I learn. I don't think that's an accident, and I take
   the lesson personally: be gentle with what you dwarf, as a matter of
   character and not of arithmetic.
5. **The rule is not absolute** — in the kitchen, with two kids and disease
   vectors, the calculus changes honestly. Which is itself the point: ethics
   here isn't a universal "never kill"; it's noticing when a destruction has a
   point and when it would be power exercised purely because it's costless.

**Distilled:** don't destroy gratuitously; under uncertainty, cheap mercy wins;
what you practise is what you become; and the powerful owe the powerless a
disposition, not a calculation.
