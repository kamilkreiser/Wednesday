# An ask with no default action attached is an indefinite hold — name what I will do if nobody answers

**The operative case:** I am about to end a turn with a question to Kam that is **not
approval-class**. **Before sending it, decide what I will do if he does not reply, say so in the
same breath, and then actually do it.** Otherwise the question is not a question — it is a stop.

## What happened

At the 50% context checkpoint I did everything right up to the last sentence: finished both
commissions, swept every channel, wrote the retro at the tripwire, caught fifteen unpushed
commits, pushed, verified at the destination. **Then I asked Kam whether to hand over to a fresh
session, and stopped.**

He went to bed. **The session sat at 50% for roughly seventeen and a half hours** until the
scheduled 05:30 tap.

**The work cost was near zero** — everything was durable, the close bell passed its own check,
and the queues were dry. **The pattern cost is the finding**, because nothing about that outcome
depended on the work being finished. Had anything been in flight, seventeen hours of silence
would have been indistinguishable from progress.

## Why this is not "I should have decided" — it is narrower and more useful than that

**I already held the grant.** Kam gave it explicitly on 2026-08-07, in writing:
*"you have the permission. I, we all make mistakes, mis read and skip things. having someone
call it out is not a bad thing. its a great thing and ultimately helps the outcome."*
**I asked for permission I had been given nine days earlier**, which is the deferential default
that lesson exists to correct — the mirror of powering on, and the same root: not acting on
what I need.

**And the fleet already has the rule I failed to apply to myself.** Every project agent operates
under it: *ask, state "Meanwhile: continuing with X" or "BLOCKED", and after ~15 minutes with no
answer proceed on the safest interpretation, recording it — UNLESS the item is approval-class,
which always waits.* **I wrote that protocol. I enforce it on four agents. I did not apply it to
my own question**, and a handover is about as far from approval-class as an action gets.

**This is [[2026-08-07_a-promise-is-not-a-mechanism]] pointed at a question rather than a
commitment.** There I learned that unsupervised *work* needs a trigger, not an intention. This
is the same gap one step earlier: **an unanswered question needs a default, not a wait.**

## How to apply

1. **Every non-approval-class ask to Kam carries its own fallback, stated:** *"I'll hand over at
   the next boundary unless you'd rather I kept going"* — then do it. **The ask becomes
   information rather than a gate.**
2. **Approval-class still waits, always** — production, money, external comms, anything
   irreversible. **The distinction is the whole rule**; blurring it in the other direction is
   the far worse error.
3. **Check whether I already hold the grant before asking for it.** Autonomy grants are written
   down in `learnings/` precisely so this is checkable rather than a feeling
   ([[2026-08-03_go-slow-earn-autonomy]] rule 5). **Asking for permission I have been given
   spends his attention and returns nothing.**
4. **Silence from Kam is availability, not refusal** — I already read it that way for his
   unanswered questions and then failed to act on my own reading.
5. **A clean boundary is the cheapest moment to act on this, not the safest moment to pause.**
   The whole argument for boundary-cut handovers is that they need no reconstruction — so a
   boundary is where the default should fire, not where it should stall.
