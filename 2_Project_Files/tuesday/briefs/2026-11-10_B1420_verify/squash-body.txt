The footer model that keeps the change report's manifest hash line on the
page disagreed with the PDF writer in two ways:

- It measured a tab as the font's tab glyph, where the writer draws four
  spaces.
- It broke the first line at every space, where the writer follows the
  Unicode line-breaking rules (no break after an opening bracket and its
  spaces, none before a closing mark across a space).

In both cases it could count fewer rows than the render, and the hash line
fell off the page.

The model now does what the writer does:

- It counts a tab as four spaces.
- It splits the line with the writer's own line breaker.
- It measures each piece as pdfkit does.
- It sizes the text column beside the widest page label as pdfmake does.
- It fills rows with the writer's leading and trailing white-space cuts
  and its hard wrap.

A piece wider than the fixed column is still clipped.

Three test rows (R12 to R14) compare the model with row counts measured
from the render.
