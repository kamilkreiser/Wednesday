The release guard judges two more certificate forms against the
development CA deny-list. A PEM block labelled PKCS7, CMS or PKCS #7
SIGNED DATA (a PKCS#7 bundle of certificates, the shape of a .p7b
file) is decoded and searched like a certificate block: a listed
certificate inside it is G2, and such a block that does not decode is
G3, as for the three certificate labels. While the list holds a
fingerprint, base64 text without PEM markers (a JSON x5c value, a bare,
line-wrapped or indented base64 run in a file, a ZIP member or UTF-16
text) is decoded at each of its four alignments and searched for a
listed DER certificate. Lines may be joined by CR LF, LF or the
characters backslash n or backslash r backslash n, with any spaces or
tabs before or after each line end; every line but the last holds at
least 16 base64 characters and the last at least 1, so a wrapped
certificate whose last line is short is read whole. With the empty list
the new rule does nothing: both publish folders read the same guard
lines as before (14 and 15 files). The docstring names the shapes still
not seen: the URL-safe alphabet, a JSON-escaped slash, any other line
end (a lone CR, an XML character reference between lines), an empty or
short line before the last line, a split across two strings, base64 of
base64, a run under 128 characters, and the empty list.
