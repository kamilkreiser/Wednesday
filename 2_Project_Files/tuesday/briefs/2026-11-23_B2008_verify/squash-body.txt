Oxpd1Writer, a new public class of the OXPd 1.x adapter, makes the two
writes of first-run step 5 over an Oxpd1Client it does not own:
RegisterAuthenticationAgent and UnregisterAuthenticationAgent of the
committed Authentication binding, and no other operation.

Every Oxpd1WriteCall is a dry run unless its Execute is set: a dry run
reads no setting, opens no connection and returns the envelope it would
send. An executed call goes through the client's internal
SendWriteAsync and its one SendAsync (the same pinned handler,
time-out, answer bounds and record as a read): ONE request carrying
HTTP Basic admin, no retry. Applied is Yes on the empty response
element; No when nothing was sent (no setting, a refused pin) or the
device refused the credentials (401, 403); Unknown on every other end.
A register's result carries its undo, the unregister of the same id
(itself a dry run), whenever Applied is Yes or Unknown: HP's
description says an unregister of an id the device does not hold
succeeds with no effect. The undo leaves the device as it was only when
that id was not registered before (a register replaces a record of the
same id).

The sign-in agent record carries no credentials and takes absolute
https addresses without user information, at most 256 characters; both
request bodies validate against HP's committed schema. Oxpd1Client's
public methods are unchanged (the two reads and Dispose) and the
reads' envelopes are byte for byte as before; its record line now
reads "OXPd 1.x call". 13 new cases against the stub on 127.0.0.1: C#
totals plus 13 on both legs, the rules count plus 2.

Steps 2, 3 and 4 of the first-run list are not built: their operations
are in no committed file under contracts/oxpd1/. The writer counts
nothing across calls; stopping after a refused password is the
caller's. No device has been reached. Nothing is deployed; nothing was
sent.
