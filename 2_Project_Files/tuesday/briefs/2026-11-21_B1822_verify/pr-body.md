**Nothing reads a real card reader yet. The library opens nothing, logs nothing, reads no clock and holds no key. The server does not reference it, so nothing of it runs in the product. It is unit code with unit tests.**

**Every golden value is PROVISIONAL.** Each was made by reading the source of the Workpath sign-in app, by three readers who agree. None was generated by running that app and none was measured on a card reader. The golden file says so in its own fields, and a test fails if its status changes while a row has no `measured_on`.

It holds four things:

1. five decoding routines, re-expressed from the Workpath sign-in app's logic (nothing copied);
2. a table of the six reader ids the app owns, with the routine each has in each of two families;
3. an assembler that puts the events of one reader into sequence order, feeds a routine and gives out finished reads;
4. the limits of a card string, and a result type that never prints the string.

## The two families

The Workpath app decodes in two ways, chosen by the printer's firmware.

- The older family: one routine each for RF IDeas (3111:15354), HP (1008:69) and Elatec (2520:1040 and 2520:784).
- The newer family: whole-buffer text for vendor 1008, a generic keyboard routine for every other reader.

**The two families do not give the same string for every input.** Of 13 comparison rows, 6 are the same and 7 differ. For the HP reader a byte 13 ends the card in the older routine and stays inside the string in the newer one. The dashes of a UUID are removed in the newer family only.

**Which family a customer's stored cards came from is not known.** The library builds both and offers three selections: older, newer, and a default that follows the design sentence (the older routine for the four enabled readers, the keyboard routine for the two conditional readers). If the selection is the wrong family for a customer's stored cards, the same card gives another string, no stored card matches, and the sign-in is refused on every such printer.

The two conditional readers (1899:21544 and 7590:272) decode nothing unless the caller switches them on.

## The assembler

Events are put in sequence order; an event ahead of a missing one is held; a missing event that does not arrive within one window (one second by default) refuses the read; a read that has not ended within one window of its earliest event is refused; more than 256 gathered characters without an end is refused; at most 32 events and 4096 bytes are held; one event of more than 4096 bytes or more than 4096 reports is refused. One read gets one refusal.

- **Time.** The caller passes the time in. It must never go back: a monotonic clock read at the call, not the wall clock. A time earlier than the last one seen is a step: a read in flight is refused once and everything pending is dropped, so no read outlives a step of the clock. A time far ahead runs the windows out. No time value makes a call throw. A caller whose times are out of call order, even by one tick, loses the read in flight to a refusal each time.
- **After a refusal.** The rest of the refused read is discarded: no card and no further refusal until the routine sees that read's end, or until one window passes with no event carrying a byte. So no part of a refused read is given out, for a read whose events keep arriving less than one window apart.
- **What a person at the printer pays.** A tap that takes more than one window from its first to its last event, or loses one event, is refused and must be repeated. After a refusal the reader gives nothing until the refused read's end arrives, or for up to one window after the last event of that read that carried a byte; a card tapped in that time is lost with no refusal of its own. Events with bytes and no end arriving less than one window apart keep the reader deaf, and the first whole card after them is swallowed.
- **Named limits, in the code's comments.** Gap detection assumes a sequence number that grows by exactly 1; switched off, a lost event is not seen. A counter that starts again looks like late copies until `Reset()`. Two reads that touch inside one window become one card. A read that stops for one window and then goes on is two reads. Two or more wrong times inside one read can end the discarding early. `Reset()` in the middle of a read lets its rest begin a new read. Whether a printer ever splits one card over two events of the whole-buffer routine has not been measured.
- `CardString` is left out by the framework's own JSON serialiser; any other serialiser, and a logger that takes an object apart, still reads it. Never hand a result to either.

## Where the library differs from the Workpath app

Each of these is a default chosen by the build's coordinator or by the builder. None is a product decision.

- **A UUID loses its dashes only in the canonical 36-character form.** The public sources of the parser the app calls also take shorter groups. Which one a printer runs has not been measured.
- **A byte above 127 is refused** where a routine turns bytes into text. The app turns such bytes into text with the platform's character set, and in the newer family it receives a string. A card whose text holds such a character works in the app and is refused here.
- **A two-byte report through the older RF IDeas routine is refused.** The app's code would fail on that report; what it does after has not been measured.
- **The first event numbered 0 is taken.** The older entry of the app drops it.
- **Vendor 1008 decodes for product 69 only.** The newer rule of the app takes any product id of that vendor; the app owns that one pair.
- **The assembler is new.** The app only drops a repeated sequence number.
- A string over 32 bytes is refused, never cut, as in the app. A blank string is no card, with the same characters counted as white space as the app's check counts by its published definition.

## What is not tested

- Anything on a printer, a card reader or a simulator. How many events a printer uses for one card, the step and the start of its sequence number, the speed of a reader and what the platform delivers in the newer family have not been measured. A reader slower than about one second per card would give no card at all under the default window.
- The comparison with real readers and the golden files of the plan's reader spike. The work package and its exit test stay open.
- Calls from several threads: the caller serialises.
