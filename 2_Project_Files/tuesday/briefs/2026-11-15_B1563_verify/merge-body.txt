Every 401 and every 403 the server answers below its audit middleware now writes one
REQUEST_DENIED audit row, whatever the method. A refused GET or HEAD used to leave no row at
all; it now writes one when its response finishes. A refused write keeps its STATE_MUTATION
row exactly as before and gains one REQUEST_DENIED row after it. A role change through
PUT /api/auth/users/:email writes one ROLE_CHANGED row with the role before and after and
who made the change; the same role again, or a role value the route ignores, writes none,
and the CONFIG_CHANGE row is unchanged.

This is the denial and role-change part of the product owner's 2026-11-11 ruling ("reads and
denials are audited"), for the two roles the product has today. A REQUEST_DENIED row holds
the method, the path, the status, the reason (unauthenticated or forbidden), the masked
email and the role, and nothing read from a header, cookie, query or body; the audit
envelope around it carries the client address and user agent, as every audit row already
does. The read hook listens for the response's finish event, so no read route has its
res.json or res.send wrapped. The new code decides nothing: it records a status that was
already answered.

Not audited yet: ordinary reads (an allowed read writes no row), the sensitive-field grants
and the audit of a granted read, the row scope of RD-930 and the assistant, export and
report scope of RD-931, which come after the capability build of RD-929. The CSRF refusal
is answered above the audit middleware and writes no row; an OPTIONS refusal writes none.

No status changes: no gate is added or moved, the role-capability inventory is unchanged,
and the open (no sign-in) window answers as before. One new service file
(backend/services/accessAudit.js), three new test files with 15 cells, and the counts file:
5651 tests across 352 suites. Nothing is deployed.
