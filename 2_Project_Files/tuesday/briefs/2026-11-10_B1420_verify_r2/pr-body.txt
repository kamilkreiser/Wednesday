The change report's footer model decides whether the first footer line fits above the manifest hash line, and clips it with dots when it does not. It disagreed with the PDF writer in three ways. In the first two the hash line could fall off the page; in the third, names were cut shorter than needed and the page label could print in fragments.

1. Tabs. The writer turns each tab into four spaces before it measures, breaks or draws text (6.45 pt a tab at 6.5 pt). The model measured the font's tab glyph (2.88 pt).
2. Line breaks. The writer breaks a line only where the Unicode line-breaking rules allow (never after an opening bracket and its spaces, never before a closing mark across a space). The model broke at every space.
3. Wide pieces and the page label. The writer gives the text column at least its widest piece and squeezes the "Page n of m" label beside it, down to one word a row. The model clipped any piece wider than a fixed column, and did not check that the label still fits the footer rows.
