# Playbook Messaging - review (2026-11-12)

Source: `2_Project_Files/fleet/state/s270/kam_playbook_messaging_0915.txt` (main message, Summary "Breakthrough Value", Apple-style).

## BLUF

- Strong, partner-centred copy. The structure (pain -> Assess/Implement/Optimize -> grow) works. It needs tightening, not a rewrite.
- 1. **Soften the claims that carry HP or partner risk:** "HP-backed" (4 uses), "reveal risks", "you do not need to become a cybersecurity company", and the lines that tie the product to revenue.
- 2. **Cut the repetition:** "Start with one customer" x3, "trust" x6, "confidence/confident" x6, and two back-to-back openers in the main message.
- 3. **Match the app's terms:** "service offers" -> "Delivery phases" (one place). Use Assess / Implement / Optimize in the Summary and Apple blocks too.

## Recommended revisions

Ordered by priority. Each one can be accepted or rejected on its own.

**(a) Risk and over-promise**

1. **All blocks, "HP-backed" (4 uses).** The main message says "HP-backed recommendations give your team a clear path to action" and "practical, HP-backed action". The Summary and Apple blocks say "HP-backed recommendations". Proposed for the main message: "recommendations grounded in HP Security Manager policy settings". In the other blocks, use "HP-aligned" or "based on HP guidance".
   Why: "backed" reads as HP endorsing or guaranteeing each recommendation. Check this claim. Keep "HP-backed" only if HP has approved that exact wording for partner-facing material.
2. **Main and Apple blocks:** "Guided assessments reveal risks" -> "Guided assessments surface likely risks".
   Why: the assessment is built from answers to questions, not a scan of the fleet. "Reveal" suggests a discovery tool, and a partner could be blamed for a risk the assessment missed.
3. **Main message:** "You do not need to become a cybersecurity company to take the next step." -> "You can take the next step without building a cybersecurity practice from scratch."
   Why: as written, it can read as "no security skills needed". That creates liability for partners who over-reach and could worry HP. The new line keeps the reassurance and drops the implied promise.
4. **Summary:** "...deepening trusted relationships without having to become a cybersecurity company." -> "...deepening trusted relationships on the HP expertise partners already have."
   Why: same risk as item 3. It also removes one of the two places this idea appears.
5. **Summary:** "a repeatable path from print security conversations to revenue" -> "a repeatable path from print security conversations to paid services".
   Why: "to revenue" sounds like a promised outcome. "Paid services" describes what the product actually enables.
6. **Apple block:** "A simpler way to turn print security needs into customer confidence—and new revenue." -> "A simpler way to turn print security needs into customer confidence—and new service opportunities."
   Why: softer on revenue. "Simpler" also invites the question "than what?". Consider "A guided way" instead.
7. **Main message:** "Recurring reviews and managed services give customers continued support" (check this claim).
   Why: the app supports recurring reviews. If it does not support managed services, write "Recurring reviews, and the managed services you build on them, give customers...".
8. **Main message:** "With a human-reviewed narrative" -> "With a narrative your team reviews before it goes out".
   Why: "human-reviewed" raises the question "who reviews it?". It can also be read as HP reviewing it. Check this claim against how the report narrative is actually produced.
9. **Main message:** "Build on the HP expertise and customer relationships you already have" (check this claim).
   Why: this is fine for HP-certified partners. If the audience includes newer partners, use "Build on your HP relationship and the customer trust you already have".

**(b) Repetition**

10. **Main message, two openers in a row.** "Your customers trust you to keep their business moving. Help them protect it." is followed shortly by "Your customers already trust you with their print environment. Become the partner they turn to for its security."
    Proposed: keep the first opening paragraph and delete the second pair of lines. Alternatively, keep the second pair as a subhead and cut "Your customers trust you to keep their business moving. Help them protect it."
    Why: two hooks in a row on the same idea weaken both.
11. **Main message:** "Start with one customer. Give them another reason to trust you." (end of the first paragraph) -> delete.
    Why: it repeats the closing line "Start with one customer. Help them see the risk..." The Apple block uses the same line a third time. Use it once per block, at the end.
12. **"trust" x6 and "confidence/confident" x6 across the blocks.**
    Proposed: in the main message, change "earn customer confidence" to "make the recommendation easy to defend". Change "turn print security uncertainty into a confident customer conversation" to "turn print security uncertainty into a clear customer conversation".
    Why: each word lands harder when it is used less.
13. **Main message:** "Build on the relationships you've earned to deliver security services your customers value—and create new opportunities for implementation and recurring revenue." The growth idea is then stated again in the "Create more ways to grow" and "Stay valuable beyond the first project" sections.
    Proposed: end the first paragraph at "...security services your customers value." and let the later sections carry the revenue point.

**(c) Clarity and rhythm**

14. **Main message:** "For your business, that creates an opportunity to deepen relationships, demonstrate expertise, and build a lasting security services practice." followed by "...gives your team a guided way to seize that opportunity."
    Proposed: "That is your opening: deeper relationships, visible expertise, and a lasting security services practice." Then "The HP Security Services Playbook gives your team a guided way in."
    Why: "opportunity" is used twice in two sentences, and "seize" is cliched.
15. **Main message:** "Audience-specific questions bring the right people into the discussion, making security conversations more approachable." -> "Questions tailored to each role bring the right people into the discussion."
    Why: shorter. "More approachable" adds little.

**(d) Product-term consistency**

16. **Main message:** "Customer status, next actions, service offers, and revenue calculators" -> "Customer status, next actions, delivery phases, and revenue calculators".
    Why: the app's menu says "Delivery phases" (ruling of 2026-11-11). This is the only place the copy says "service offers".
17. **Summary and Apple blocks:** name the method there too. In the Summary: "Guided assessments, HP-aligned recommendations, and executive reports across Assess, Implement, and Optimize help teams..." In the Apple block: "One guided experience: Assess, Implement, Optimize."
    Why: Assess / Implement / Optimize currently appears only once, in the main message. Short-form readers never see the app's own structure.
18. **Apple block:** consider adding "Good, Better, Best options for every finding" after "a clear path from findings to action".
    Why: this is a concrete differentiator, and it is missing from both short blocks. Check this claim: "for every finding" is only true if every finding has all three tiers. Otherwise write "Good, Better, Best options".

## Light-touch notes (optional)

- "print security uncertainty" is abstract. "unanswered print security questions" is more concrete.
- "Make delivery more repeatable." is the weakest section opener. Consider "Deliver the same way, every time."
- "Relevant recommendations can also open conversations about HP security software, authentication, secure print, hardware, and device refresh." This is useful for partners. Keep it out of any customer-facing derivative, because it reads as upsell.
- "Build a practice with a shared methodology instead of starting from scratch for every customer." Good line. It might work better as the final line of "Make delivery more repeatable".
- Apple block: "Built on your HP expertise." conflicts with item 9 if newer partners are in scope.
- Use the same dash style everywhere. The em dashes have no spaces, which is fine; keep them that way in every block.

## What works

- The section openers ("Give sellers the confidence...", "Show customers a clear reason to act.", "Connect technical findings to business decisions.") are strong and easy to scan.
- "Each finding traces back to the answer and rule behind it" is a real, credible differentiator. Keep it, and move it higher if you can.
- "Recommendations map findings to named HP Security Manager policy settings, including where to find them and what to set" is concrete and believable. It is the best proof point in the piece.
- The Apple-style headline, "The trust you've earned. The security business you can build.", is good. Keep it as is.
- The "Create more ways to grow each account" chain (assessment -> paid discovery -> implementation -> recurring) explains the business model in one breath.
