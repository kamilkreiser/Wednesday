## What this adds

A library, `src/Datasec.Suite.Cards` (16 source files, no package, no project reference), and its tests, `tests/Datasec.Suite.Cards.Tests` (52 test methods, 131 test cells; the solution now runs 132 tests on both legs).

**Nothing reads a real card reader yet. The library opens nothing, logs nothing, reads no clock and holds no key. The server does not reference it, so nothing of it runs in the product. It is unit code with unit tests.**

**Every golden value is PROVISIONAL.** Each was made by reading the source of the Workpath sign-in app, by three readers who agree (the drafter of the work, the builder, the reviewer). None was generated by running that app and none was measured on a card reader. The golden file says so in its own fields, and a test fails if its status changes while a row has no `measured_on`.

It holds four things:

1. five decoding routines, re-expressed from the Workpath sign-in app's logic (nothing copied);
2. a table of the six reader ids the app owns, with the routine each has in each of two families;
3. an assembler that puts the events of one reader into sequence order, feeds a routine and gives out finished reads;
4. the limits of a card string, and a result type that never prints the string.

## The two families

The Workpath app decodes in two ways, chosen by the printer's firmware.

- The older family: one routine each for RF IDeas (3111:15354), HP (1008:69) and Elatec (2520:1040 and 2520:784).
- The newer family: whole-buffer text for vendor 1008, a generic keyboard routine for every other reader.

**The two families do not give the same string for every input.** Of 13 comparison rows, 6 are the same and 7 differ. For the HP reader a byte 13 ends the card in the older routine and stays inside the string in the newer one. The dashes of a UUID are removed in the newer family only.

**Which family a customer's stored cards came from is not known.** The library builds both and offers three selections: older, newer, and a default that follows the design sentence (the older routine for the four enabled readers, the keyboard routine for the two conditional readers). If the selection is the wrong family for a customer's stored cards, the same card gives another string, no stored card matches, and the sign-in is refused on every such printer.

The two conditional readers (1899:21544 and 7590:272) decode nothing unless the caller switches them on.

## Where the library differs from the Workpath app

Each of these is a default chosen by the build's coordinator or by the builder. None is a product decision.

- **A UUID loses its dashes only in the canonical 36-character form.** The public sources of the parser the app calls also take shorter groups. Which one a printer runs has not been measured.
- **A byte above 127 is refused** where a routine turns bytes into text. The app turns such bytes into text with the platform's character set, and in the newer family it receives a string. A card whose text holds such a character works in the app and is refused here.
- **A two-byte report through the older RF IDeas routine is refused.** The app's code would fail on that report; what it does after has not been measured.
- **The first event numbered 0 is taken.** The older entry of the app drops it.
- **Vendor 1008 decodes for product 69 only.** The newer rule of the app takes any product id of that vendor; the app owns that one pair.
- **The assembler is new.** The app only drops a repeated sequence number. Here: events are put in order; an event ahead of a missing one is held; a missing event that does not arrive within one second refuses the read; a read that began and has not ended within one second is refused; more than 256 gathered characters without an end is refused; at most 32 events and 4096 bytes are held; one event of more than 4096 bytes or more than 4096 reports is refused.
- A string over 32 bytes is refused, never cut, as in the app. A blank string is no card, with the same characters counted as white space as the app's check counts by its published definition.

## Measured in review at this head, and not right yet

- **Time that goes back stops every window.** The caller passes the time in. If that time steps back, or was once far ahead, no read is refused by time until the clock passes its latest value again, and a read without its end is put in front of the next card: one card string made of two reads. `Reset()` does not heal it.
- **The rest of a refused read is given out as a card.** After the assembler refuses a read (a missing event with more than 32 events held, a read slower than one second, the cap), the events that follow are taken as a new read, although they can be the end of the same card. Measured: one event lost early in a card of 20 characters, delivered one report per event, gives a refusal and then a card of the last 2 characters. With a whole card in one event this does not happen. The comment that says no part of a refused read is given out is not true yet.
- A reader that decodes nothing answers every event with a refusal: one tap can be 22 refusals.
- Gap detection assumes the sequence number grows by exactly 1. With another step no card is given out. The step on the newer printers has not been measured; the caller can switch the check off.
- Twelve stated behaviours have no test that can fail yet (shift on a digit, the signs of the usage table, a key held across events, the cap at its edge, and others). Each is right at this head: an independent model agrees with the library on 1,274,926 generated inputs.

## What is not tested

- Anything on a printer, a card reader or a simulator. How many events a printer uses for one card, the step and the start of its sequence number, the speed of a reader and what the platform delivers in the newer family have not been measured.
- The comparison with real readers and the golden files of the plan's reader spike. The work package and its exit test stay open.
- Calls from several threads: the caller serialises.
